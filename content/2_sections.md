## The Fire, Introduction and Thesis

> "Pronounce the name: Spokhin, not Spo-keen, as you learned in school. Where is Spokane located? Ask your banker. No, don't, listen to what two jokers, black comedians, said ten-tens of years ago in one of our café chantants: 'Where is Spokane located' asks Rastus to Sam. The answer was: 'Somewhere in Holland'. Loud applause, everyone understood the joke. Because enterprising banks from the Netherlands were godparents at the birth of the new town in the Northwest of America."
>
> -- “OVER STATES. SPOKANE "THE PLAYGROUND OF THE NATION." Originally a trading post. — Dutch bankers helped make the city great. — The traditional fire. — Wheat, apples, metals, and "white coal.” Algemeen Handelsblad (Amsterdam), evening edition, January 31, 1931. P. den Hengst en Zoon [etc.]. Accessed via Delpher (KB C 21).

- August 4th 1899 fire destroying 32 blocks in downtown Spokane

> "Such is Chicago’s great fire of 1871; for while Spokane was not as large as Chicago at the time of the fires, yet, the total destruction of the business centers was a feature of both; and like Chicago, the city of Spokane Falls has already begun to see that the fire was a blessing in disguise, for a city of magnificent brick and granite business blocks has begun to grow, where before were small buildings inadequate to the needs of a city with such a brilliant future."
>
> -- The Northwest Magazine. “Chicago’s Great Fire of 1871.” April 1890, 14.

- Rebuilding involved financial leaders raising the price of downtown lots by 25 percent, driving out the Chinese ownership, gambling houses, single men's dwellings and saloons across the river.

> "Sent to analyze the status of Holland investments in Spokane in 1896, Isaac C. Jolies concluded that his ' company owned 25 percent of the city- the best 25 percent, since most of the property was located near the center. Although the acquisitions had not been systematic, Jolies feared that if the fact became known "western radicals" might throw the Dutch out as foreign oppressors."
>
> -- Fahey, John. “When the Dutch Owned Spokane.” The Pacific Northwest Quarterly 72, no. 1 (1981): 2.


## The Cape Era Diamond Boom

> "The land was under Dutch colonial control from 1652 to 1806, except for a period of British control from 1795 to 1803 due to the Napoleonic Wars.3 The Netherlands ceded the land to Britain in 1806—or perhaps the British merely occupied it until the Dutch gave up—and diamonds were discovered at Kimberley in 1867."
>
> -- Murdock, Michelle M. “Polishing Up the Diamond Trade: How to Revitalize the Kimberley Process.” Journal of Corporation Law 46, no. 2 (2020): 463–81. 148694740.

> "The yield was initially disappointing, but a year later, the "claims" (some 3,600 in the Kimberley mine alone) were consolidated by Cecil Rhodes and Berney Barnato, who succeeded in taking first place. De Beers Consolidated Diamond Mines Limited was founded, and in 1870, the first shipment of African diamonds arrived in Amsterdam. A veritable "boom" then developed. Due to the demand, especially from America for the polished product, more and more cutters were trained." 
>
> -- “Under the Imperial Crown Memories of the clover shop.” NRC Handelsblad (Rotterdam), daily edition, July 1, 1975. Nieuwe Rotterdamse Courant. Accessed via Delpher (Koninklijke Bibliotheek).

> "It was during those days (after 1867) that the great "run" broke out in South Africa. Diamond fields were discovered. Adventurers from all over the world left their homes and farms to devote themselves to the risky game of gilding. In midwinter 1870, the first diamond arrived in Amsterdam. An unprecedented period of luxury began. In 1872, 1,200 workers received 7 to 8 million in wages. Everything shifted to the "trade."
>
> -- “THE DISAPPEARING GHETTO X. THE PLANTATION” Nieuw Israelietisch Weekblad (Amsterdam), daily edition, April 2, 1948. J.B. de Mesquita. Accessed via Delpher (Universiteitsbibliotheek Amsterdam, ROS VV 120).

> "Wages in the diamond industry, already higher than in other trades, shot up to new heights. Weekly wages of 200 guilders (also known as Dutch florins, abbreviated hereafter as Dfl.) and more became common, and raises of between 100 and 200 percent are reported in 1872. Comparable wages in other trades, such as the printing business, were 7 or 8 guilders a week..."
>
> -- Veenendaal, Augustus J. Slow Train to Paradise: How Dutch Investment Helped Build American Railroads. Stanford University Press, 1996.

> In 1895, ten years after the establishment of the compound system, Kimberley’s seven registered diamond mines (excluding the alluvial digging sites) employed a daily average of 1,698 white men, as well as 7,808 black mineworkers, all of them living inside the compounds. d Mines. By the late 1880s, diamond mining relied on a workforce that consisted of unskilled, black migrant labourers who worked in underground galleries. These men, sometimes traveling from thousands of kilometers away, were placed in special encampments named compounds. For long periods of time, black mineworkers had no contact with the outside world. In colonialist discourse, this full control over black bodies was intended to prevent diamond theft and alcohol consumption. Various missionaries considered these compounds the perfect places to“civilize” Africans.
> 
> -- Azevedo, Rafael de, and Tijl Vanneste. “‘The Very Soul Must Be Held in Bondage!’: Alice Victoria Kinloch’s Critical Examination of South Africa’s Diamond-Mining Compounds.” International Review of Social History 69, no. 3 (2024): 383–410.

- "Hut Tax" being instituted in 1878 to force Black land owners into debt and the diamond mine compounds

- By the 1880s, the US is the largest client of diamonds on the global market.

- While the diamond boom ended in 1873, that was also the year American railroad loans were introduced in Amsterdam.

## Dutch Investment in American Railroad Bonds

> "With Dutch capital and Dutch "brains," the wholly (100%) American cities of Norfolk, Virginia, Spokane , Washington, and Port Arthur, Texas were built. A more or less Dutch-owned railway connects Port Arthur with Kansas City... Numerous "granddaughter colonies" grew her "kleindochter-kolonies”.
>
> -- Hoefer, F. A., and J. van Hinte. Historical Atlas of the Netherlands. ’s-Gravenhage: Nijhoff, 1934.

> "A common form of gambling was speculation in securities on the Bourse (exchange). It is not unreasonable to assume that the boom in American "rails," with their promise of quick gains, was at least in part fueled by the money being made so easily in the diamond industry."
>
> -- Veenendaal, Augustus J. Slow Train to Paradise: How Dutch Investment Helped Build American Railroads. Stanford University Press, 1996.

- American railroad bonds being viewed as a "high-risk, high reward" investment with the newly rich diamond worker classes. The bonds are written about with skepticism from the established financial classes and syndicates are formed to try and regulate and inform investments. 

> "Dutch capital was second only to British capital among the foreign investors in fashioning the extensive railroad network in mid- and late nineteenth-century America... Clearly, the railroads dominated Dutch investments in the United States in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries"
> 
> -- Wilkins, Mira. “Dutch Multinational Enterprises in the United States: A Historical Summary.” The Business History Review 79, no. 2 (2005): 193–273.

> "The Dutch financier is said to have a generally good nose for investing his capital. If this is true, then much can certainly be expected from the land to which all of America now eagerly awaits: the far northwest, the state of Washington, and the neighboring parts of Montana and Oregon; the region that, with a proud yet somewhat peculiarly inflected name, likes to call itself the Inland Empire . It may not yet have the significance of a "rich" (country), with its still sparse population, but it looks like it won't be a powerful and powerful area for much longer. But why "Inland"? Its significance will largely be due to the fact that it isn't an inland area, but possesses magnificent and excellently situated ports. Currently, almost all the cities in this area are still "port cities." Inland lies only one large city, Spokane . Spokane began to resemble a small town in 1881, when the first railroad, the Northern Pacific, arrived... In this Spokane now, since For years, Dutch capital has been established, particularly in the form of mortgage banks, as is the case in the port cities, notably Seattle and Portland. The director of the oldest Dutch mortgage bank, Mr. Insinger, is chairman of the Spokane Chamber of Commerce . It seems that now, after the recent increase, the number of Dutch mortgage banks in these regions should be considered quite sufficient for the time being."
>
> -- "Impressions of America by J.P. Niermeyer VII. THE INLAND EMPIRE." Het nieuws van den dag: kleine courant, Dag edition, January 27, 1913, no. 13230, KB c226.
